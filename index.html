Perception · decisions · interpretability · security

Every decision is a contest. I study who wins.

Eyes against ears. Memory against the moment. Your instruction against one hidden inside an image. Minds and models settle these conflicts constantly, mostly out of sight.

I work on how vision and multimodal AI settles them, how to read the result from the inside, and how to stop someone from rigging it.

Where I've met it

Four times I watched the wrong side win

Different jobs, different data, the same failure. Each one is why I now care more about how a decision is reached than about the accuracy of any single part.

Aptiv · inside the car voicevsface

The yawn

A cabin monitor heard a yawn and flagged a tired driver. The camera showed someone upright and talking animatedly. The voice reached the decision first, so the voice won.

Fast evidence is not the same as good evidence.

What I built: real-time speech emotion models (85% on IEMOCAP), an adaptive soundscape that responds in under 100 ms, and the Android Automotive audio stack they ran on.

BITS Pilani · wearables priorvssignal

The heart or the noise

In wearable ECG data, the outliers were bending the very statistics meant to catch them. The model's idea of "normal" kept winning arguments it should have lost.

A prior can be corrupted by the thing it is meant to detect.

What I built: a robust distance, ARMD, that beat classical Mahalanobis by 25%, and an LSTM autoencoder that separates four kinds of temporal anomaly. Code.

L&T · engineering diagrams textvsgeometry

The valve that moved

On a piping diagram, a label a few millimetres out of place changes what the plant does. The OCR read every word correctly and still misread the diagram.

Every part can be right while the whole is wrong.

What I built: a detection and OCR pipeline that beat template matching by 15–20%, then linked symbols to meaning, cutting human classification errors by about 30%.

Pragya AI Lab · robots EnglishvsHindi, Tamil…

The instruction that arrived empty

Two different Hindi instructions can reach a motion model as the same sequence of tokens. The English-shaped interface wins before the contest even starts, and the robot moves the same way for both.

Sometimes the loser is erased before anyone votes.

What I'm building: alignment of Hindi, Bengali, Tamil and Telugu embeddings directly into the space a humanoid motion model listens to, so instructions never pass through English.

Tone against words

When the words say "I'm fine" and the voice says otherwise, audio language models usually believe the words. A 2026 KAIST study traced where that bias lives for literal speech in English, and left tone, emotion and other languages open. I'm mapping it for tone of voice across English, Hindi and Tamil, with open models.
