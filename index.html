The question I keep landing on

A cabin monitoring system raised a driver fatigue warning off a yawn. The interior camera, at the same timestamp, showed the person sitting upright, mid conversation, animated.

The audio channel was not wrong about the sound. It was wrong about the state, and nothing in the pipeline could hold both facts at once. I have hit the same shape of problem in ECG anomaly detection, in engineering diagrams, and now in Indic language conditioning. It keeps producing the same three questions.

  1. Representation. How should multimodal context be represented so a model can reason across modalities, rather than vote between per channel scores computed independently?
  2. Robustness. What does fusion look like when channels actively conflict, degrade, or drop out entirely, as opposed to the clean paired data most benchmarks assume?
  3. Time. How do long range temporal dependencies survive being carried across modalities that sample the world at different rates and with different latencies?

Indic instructions that a motion model never receives

Apr 2026 – now

Research contributor, Pragya AI Lab, BITS Pilani Goa · PragyaVLA · supervisor Amitava Das

Finding. English centric text interfaces can collapse semantically distinct Indic instructions into identical token sequences. When that happens, a language conditioned motion model is not failing at grounding. It never received the distinction in the first place, so every downstream fix is aimed at the wrong stage.

  • Built Indic language embedding representations and alignment inside a multilingual Indic embedding space, so Hindi, Bengali, Tamil and Telugu instructions condition a pretrained humanoid motion generation model directly, without translating to English at inference.
  • Worked on mapping frozen multilingual text representations into the conditioning geometry a pretrained motion diffusion model expects, which is where the representational mismatch actually bites.
  • Contributed to the PragyaVLA research codebase, and built Hugging Face Spaces so the conditioning behaviour is reproducible by people who are not running the training stack.

Open questions I am actually stuck on

Dated, and revised when I learn something. If you work on any of these I would rather hear that I am wrong than not hear.

Nov 2026

Is the Indic collapse a tokenizer artefact or a geometry one?

Two instructions can merge because the tokenizer gives them the same pieces, or because the encoder maps different pieces to nearly the same point in conditioning space. These call for different fixes, and I do not yet have a clean way to attribute a given collapse to one or the other without retraining.

Oct 2026

What does a benchmark for conflicting modalities look like?

Standard multimodal benchmarks are built from agreeing channels, so a model that silently ignores one modality scores well. I want a protocol where conflict and dropout are first class conditions with ground truth, not corruptions applied at eval time, and I am not convinced synthetic conflict measures the same thing as the real kind.

Sep 2026

Does predicting in representation space help when channels disagree?

Joint embedding predictive architectures avoid reconstructing pixels or waveforms, which is appealing when one channel is unreliable. Whether predicting in latent space actually preserves a conflict, or quietly averages it away, is the thing I want to test rather than assume.
